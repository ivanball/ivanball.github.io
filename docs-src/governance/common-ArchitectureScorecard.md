# MMCA.Common: Architecture Scorecard

> **Canonical, version-controlled scorecard for this repo**: the single source of truth for MMCA.Common's architecture scores. Scored against the rubric at [`ArchitectureEvaluationCriteria.md`](ArchitectureEvaluationCriteria.md) (rubric version 2, [ADR-110](../adr/110-rubric-v2-category-realignment.md)); framework-wide facts (version, packages, ADR range, fitness counts) live in [`FACTS.md`](https://github.com/ivanball/MMCA.Common/blob/main/FACTS.md); the ranked open work lives in the [remediation backlog](common-RemediationBacklog.md). The cross-repo Common-vs-ADC-vs-Store narrative lives once in the workspace-internal `Docs/Architecture/CrossRepoComparison.md` (not published).

_Evidence as of 2026-11-23 at v1.315.0, git HEAD `4816b36d` (clean tree). Rubric version 2._
_Facts re-verified 2026-11-24 against MMCA.Common v1.318.0 (MMCA.Common HEAD `72fb9b96`); scores, indices and bands are unchanged from the evidence date above and move only at the next re-score._

## At a glance

- **Maturity index** = Σ(maturity×weight) ÷ Σ(weight×4) = 319 ÷ 328 = **97.3%**
- **Implementation index** = Σ(impl×weight) ÷ Σ(weight×10) = 698 ÷ 820 = **85.1%**
- **Weaker axis:** Implementation (execution quality), by ~12.2 points.
- **Maturity band:** 4 categories sit below Maturity 4 (§1, §17, §30 and §31, all at 3), 9 weighted gap points; see the [backlog](common-RemediationBacklog.md).
- **Implementation band:** 18 categories sit at Implementation 8 or below (§1, §4, §5, §10, §11, §12, §13, §16, §17, §20, §23, §24, §27, §29, §30, §31, §33, §34), 40 weighted gap points against the scheduling target of 9; see the [backlog](common-RemediationBacklog.md).

MMCA.Common is strongest where its conventions are executable: 30 of 34 categories sit at Maturity 4, most of them on build-breaking gates (dual-enforced layering, fitness functions shipped as a package and re-run in every consumer, required cross-browser, accessibility, performance and consumer-canary checks, a CI-gated `FACTS.md`). The remaining gap sits on the Implementation axis, and most of its weight is structural rather than neglected: deployment execution, production SLOs and dashboards, realistic-volume load evidence, cost right-sizing and the consent and residency process belong to the consuming apps, which is why §17, §29, §30 and §31 hold at 8 or below. §16 AI-Native holds at Implementation 8 on two partial criteria, guardrails and evaluation; the two in-repo levers named for them shipped in v1.317.0 (`Ai:RequireGuardrail` is now satisfied only by a registration marked `IPromptInjectionGuardrail`, so the PII redactor alone no longer passes, `AI/DependencyInjection.cs:215-227`; golden-replay cases carry the `PromptContractHash` they were recorded against, `AI.Testing/GoldenReplayCase.cs:19`), and the response blocklist still ships empty by design (`AI/Guardrails/ContentPolicySettings.cs:23`); the score moves only at the next re-score. Forms and Front-End Performance hold at Implementation 8 on consumer-owned or residual gaps. Several other options are recorded as accepted rather than scheduled, and the remaining implementation-band categories, Messaging among them, have no lever identified yet.

## Top 5 risks

1. **FinOps execution and governance live downstream**, §31 (impl 8, maturity 3; a documented accepted cap per the backlog's *Deliberate and accepted* section): the framework ships real cost levers (an OTel `Telemetry:TracesSampleRatio` sampler knob, the outbox per-message log at Debug, `OutboxPollFilterProcessor`, and the default-ON probe-telemetry filter plus its span processor, `Aspire/Extensions.Telemetry.cs:100,131`, that keep health-probe traces and their SQL/Redis children out of ingestion), but right-sizing, reversible scale-events and per-service cost attribution are consumer/IaC concerns a library cannot perform. The one enforced cost convention is the shipped `CostTagConventionTestsBase` attribution gate that ADC and Store subclass in CI; nothing enforces cost discipline at review time.
   - _Remediation:_ The in-repo telemetry-cost half is done. What remains is structural: the consumer apps must demonstrate measured right-sizing and per-service cost attribution that the framework's knobs feed, and cost discipline would have to be enforced beyond the attribution tag (the pull-request template and `CONTRIBUTING.md` carry no cost item).
   - _Expected delta:_ +1 maturity on §31 (3→4, +2 weighted) needs cost enforced end to end by CI and review; 8→9 needs the consumer-side right-sizing and attribution evidence.

2. **Deployment gating is verified only downstream**, §17 (maturity 3, impl 8, weight 2): a reference `samples/deployment` Bicep/OIDC sample + `DEPLOYMENT.md` ship and are compile-checked on every pull request, but that job is not a required context, and a library cannot self-deploy, so real CD-to-Azure lives in consumers; keyless nuget.org publishing under ADR-053 and the reviewer-gated `release` environment harden the release identity, not the deploy axis.
   - _Remediation:_ None scheduled in-repo. A scheduled Azure smoke-deploy of the `samples/deployment` sample is recorded as accepted rather than adopted (new Azure credentials, ongoing spend and a new CI failure path for a library, while ADC and Store prove the same Bicep/OIDC path in production; see the backlog's *Deliberate and accepted*).
   - _Expected delta:_ +1 impl on §17 (+2 weighted), and the maturity rung, remain available downstream.

3. **Compliance governance is consumer-owned; the in-repo guard verifies a mechanism, not the category**, §30 (impl 8, maturity 3, weight 2): the headline §30 red flags are closed in-framework (the `IAnonymizable` erasure extension point, AES-256-GCM at rest, and the shipped, tested `PiiRedactor` masking `[Pii]` members before logs/telemetry), and `PiiErasureContractFitnessTests` forces a `[Pii]` type through `PiiRedactor` + `IAnonymizable`. `PiiConventionTests` adds a non-vacuous marking rule (`Governance/PiiConventionTests.cs:10-14`), `PRIVACY.md` ships in-repo, and the CSV export is fail-closed (`EntityControllerBase.cs:232,273`). The governing *process* (consent capture, lawful basis, residency verification) is delegated to consumers: consent appears in Domain and Application only as the opt-in terms-acceptance stamp (`Domain/Auth/ILegalAcceptingUser.cs:11`, `Application/Auth/Legal/LegalAcceptancePolicy.cs:27`) and has zero matches in Infrastructure, and `DataResidencyTestsBase` has only its self-test probe in-repo, so the category as a whole is not automatically enforced.
   - _Remediation:_ The in-repo mechanism is done; further maturity movement needs the consumer apps to carry the personal-data inventory + DSAR/consent/residency process the framework's `[Pii]`/`IAnonymizable` extension points feed (ADR-005).
   - _Expected delta:_ +1 maturity on §30 (3→4, +2 weighted) is a consumer-side governance lift, not an in-repo one; the framework half is complete.

4. **Security holds at Implementation 8 on criteria that are unmet by decision**, §11 (maturity 4, impl 8, weight 3, implPriority 3): authentication, authorization, rate limiting, brute-force protection, PBKDF2 hashing and security headers are strong and centralized, but the rubric-v2 written threat model is absent repo-wide and is recorded as not scheduled work by user decision, authorization is RBAC with capability indirection plus opt-in ownership rather than a resource/attribute policy engine. Two internal trust gaps sit beside it: the broker consumer restores user id, roles and tenant from unsigned `MMCA-*` message headers (`Infrastructure/Messaging/Consumers/ConsumerOriginRestore.cs:45-52`, `Shared/Messaging/MessageHeaders.cs:31`), so any party able to publish to the broker can assert roles (signing them is recorded as accepted, not scheduled); and in-cluster gRPC forwards the user JWT over deliberate, documented cleartext h2c (`Grpc/DependencyInjection.cs:56,109-112`), where the shipped service identity is an opt-in app-layer HS256 token over one key shared by every service (`Grpc/Authentication/ServiceTokenServiceCollectionExtensions.cs:35,66,148,151`, `ServiceTokenSettings.cs:13,30`), adopted by both Store (`Sales.Service/Program.cs:147`) and ADC (`Engagement.Service/Program.cs:232,238`). Tenant resolution defaults to claim-only (`Infrastructure/Persistence/Tenancy/TenancySettings.cs:61-62`), with the header an explicit opt-in (`:18-19`) and a shipped `TenantResolutionTestsBase` (`Source/Hosting/MMCA.Common.Testing/Conformance/TenantResolutionTestsBase.cs:27`). `SECURITY.md:87-94` documents the shipped `AddAzureKeyVaultConfiguration` helper (a no-op unless `KeyVault:Uri` is set) and the sample's Key Vault URL plus managed-identity binding.
   - _Remediation:_ Make the in-cluster service identity per-service or transport-level rather than one shared signing key. Signing the broker origin headers and the written threat model stay accepted, unscheduled gaps.
   - _Expected delta:_ The service-identity lever protects Implementation 8; 8→9 (+3 weighted) would also need the threat model and a resource/attribute policy engine.

5. **Production continuity evidence is consumer-owned**, §29 (maturity 4, impl 8, weight 3, implPriority 3): failure isolation is fitness-enforced and the in-repo restore drill is a build gate with a measured baseline, but the framework's own guide records production RTO/RPO, restores and SLO alerting as the consumer's (`Website/docs-src/guides/common-RESILIENCE.md:5,31`), with the SLO section an unfilled template (`:95`).
   - _Remediation:_ Tested restores against real cloud backups, RTO/RPO per service and measured production SLOs in the consumer IaC; nothing further is schedulable in-repo.
   - _Expected delta:_ +1 impl on §29 (+3 weighted) is a consumer-side lift.

## Top 5 strengths

1. **Dual-enforced Clean Architecture dependency rule (compile-time + fitness functions)**, §3 (impl 9): `Source/Build/MMCA.Common.LayerEnforcement.targets:20-341` fails the build on layer violation (`BeforeTargets ResolveProjectReferences`); `LayerDependencyTests.cs:18` and `DomainPurityTests.cs:9` re-enforce Shared→Domain→Application→Infra→API/UI and Domain framework-freedom via the metadata-based architecture rules. Reinforced by §34 (impl 8).
   - _Remediation:_ Preserve by keeping both gates wired into CI and adding a regression test whenever a new layer/package is introduced; document the targets file in CLAUDE.md. To push the residual point, narrow the broader base-controller/UoW surfaces noted in §1.
   - _Expected delta:_ Preserves impl 9 on §3; protects §34 from regression.

2. **Reference-quality test strategy (define-once fitness functions + an enforced coverage gate)**, §14 (impl 9): a right-shaped pyramid (**~10,000** `[Fact]/[Theory]`) with executable architecture fitness functions (LayerDependency, DomainPurity, MicroserviceExtraction, DependencyVersion, PiiConvention, PiiErasureContract, AggregateConvention, EventVersioningConvention, FrameworkSanity, **Specification**, **SliceCohesion**) authored once in the shipped `MMCA.Common.Testing.Architecture` package (**186 methods across 74 abstract bases**, `FACTS.md:98`) and re-run identically across all three repos (ADR-015), a zero-discovery min-tests guard, a shipped reusable E2E package, and a **68.3% line-coverage floor that fails the build** (`ci.yml:624`).
   - _Remediation:_ Preserve by keeping the floor green and ratcheting it upward over releases; add mutation testing on the Core tier to reach a 10.
   - _Expected delta:_ Holds §14 at impl 9; mutation testing on the Core tier is the remaining path toward 10; protects §28.

3. **Supply-chain hardening with a license-pin build gate, a blocking SBOM, and Dependabot held-package guards**, §32 (impl 9, weight 3): `Directory.Build.props:20-22` (lock files, NuGetAudit, audit=all); `Directory.Packages.props:164-167` (no messaging library is referenced: the broker transports are framework code over the vendor SDKs, ADR-118); `DependencyVersionTestsBase.cs:12-18` fails the build on any `MassTransit*` PackageVersion (an outright ban since v1.316.0, replacing the earlier v8 pin and major-version gate); 68 `packages.lock.json` (30 under `Source/`); CycloneDX SBOM a hard release gate plus signed build-provenance attestations (`release.yml:111,157`); and a committed `.github/dependabot.yml:58-89` with `semver-major` ignores on every held package, so the pin discipline lives in config as well as the test.
   - _Remediation:_ Automate the coordinated consumer version sweep (currently policy) to close the one remaining minor gap.
   - _Expected delta:_ Automating the sweep nudges §32 toward fully exemplary; preserves impl 9.

4. **Centralized, alg-pinned security with shipped anti-automation defenses and PBKDF2 hashing**, §11 (impl 8): `WebApplicationBuilderExtensions.Authentication.cs:249,265` pins ValidAlgorithms per RS256/HS256 (defeats alg-confusion) with full issuer/audience/lifetime/signing-key validation, `:110` extends the RS256 pin to the JWKS-forwarded path; `WebApplicationBuilderExtensions.RateLimiting.cs:76` rate-limit bypass for health/.well-known/grpc; `LoginProtectionService.cs:72,127,199` adds exponential brute-force lockout + per-IP registration limit (ADR-029); `PasswordHasher.cs:34,37,106` hashes with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 (210,000 iterations for new hashes, with the legacy 600,000-iteration shape still verified and reported for rehash, PBKDF2-only, `FixedTimeEquals`, ADR-032): all returning `Result.Failure` rather than throwing.
   - _Remediation:_ Keep the CI vulnerable-package gate green.
   - _Expected delta:_ Preserves impl 8; the threat model that 8→9 needs is an accepted, unscheduled gap (see risk 4).

5. **Architecture-test-enforced microservices extraction boundaries + event versioning**, §7 (maturity 4, impl 9): ADR-006 (database-per-service), transport-agnostic `IMessageBus.cs:29`, and the shared `Rules/Layering/ArchitectureRules.Transport.cs:26` rule (subclassed through `Bases/Layering/MicroserviceExtractionTestsBase.cs:14` in `MicroserviceExtractionTests.cs:11`, run at `ci.yml:164` under a required check) forbidding Domain, Application and Shared any dependency on a broker client (RabbitMQ, Azure Service Bus, Amazon SQS/SNS, MassTransit), Grpc or Protobuf (`:14-23`). Reinforced by §6's `BaseIntegrationEvent.SchemaVersion` (ADR-010) + `EventVersioningConventionTests`, so cross-service contracts carry an explicit version, and by ADR-090 upcasting for retired contracts. The rubric-v2 modernization-patterns criterion is met in shipped docs (`Grpc/DependencyInjection.cs:21-24,77-78`, `007-grpc-extraction.md:33`, `008-service-extraction-topology.md:60`).
   - _Remediation:_ Preserve the naming by keeping the ACL and Strangler Fig wording in the Grpc XML docs and in ADR-007/008 whenever either is revised (no drift gate watches the naming itself); to push the residual point, demonstrate one end-to-end lift-out (the Notifications module) in a sample to convert "extractable by design" into "extracted and proven".
   - _Expected delta:_ 9→10 on §7 needs a proven extraction, which is structurally consumer-side.

The denominators are ×4 and ×10. The backlog's scheduling target is 9: the 9→10 rung is recognition at re-score time, not scheduled work, so the implementation band ranks only categories at Implementation 8 or below.
- **Axis-gap calibration:** §17 is *mature-but-execution-deferred* (the mechanism ships in-repo, deployment proof lives downstream); §12, §13 and §29 hold Maturity 4 with Implementation 8 because load testing, dashboards, production SLOs and real-backup RTO/RPO are deployer-owned; §30 and §31 are the inverse (in-repo implementation leads a maturity capped by consumer-owned governance and cost process). Together these are the structural source of the gap between the two indices.
- **A FLAG never shrinks the denominators:** a category whose proposed move is refuted on the adversarial pass is carried at its adjudicated score.
