    Architecture governance

    MMCA.Common: Architecture Scorecard

    Canonical, version-controlled scorecard for this repo: the single source of truth for MMCA.Common's architecture scores. Scored against the rubric at ArchitectureEvaluationCriteria.md (rubric version 2, ADR-110); framework-wide facts (version, packages, ADR range, fitness counts) live in FACTS.md; the ranked open work lives in the remediation backlog. The cross-repo Common-vs-ADC-vs-Store narrative lives once in the workspace-internal Docs/Architecture/CrossRepoComparison.md (not published).

    Evidence as of 2026-10-01 at v1.218.0, git HEAD f93bc6e2 (dirty tree). Rubric version 2.

    At a glance

    • Maturity index = Σ(maturity×weight) ÷ Σ(weight×4) = 317 ÷ 328 = 96.6%
    • Implementation index = Σ(impl×weight) ÷ Σ(weight×10) = 705 ÷ 820 = 86.0%
    • Weaker axis: Implementation (execution quality), by ~10.6 points.
    • Maturity band: 4 categories sit below Maturity 4 (§31 at 2, §1, §17 and §30 at 3), 11 weighted gap points; see the backlog.
    • Implementation band: 15 categories sit at Implementation 8 or below (§1, §4, §5, §11, §12, §13, §17, §20, §23, §27, §29, §30, §31, §33, §34), 33 weighted gap points against the scheduling target of 9; see the backlog.

    MMCA.Common is strongest where its conventions are executable: 30 of 34 categories sit at Maturity 4, most of them on build-breaking gates (dual-enforced layering, fitness functions shipped as a package and re-run in every consumer, required cross-browser, accessibility, performance and consumer-canary checks, a CI-gated FACTS.md). The remaining gap sits on the Implementation axis, and most of its weight is structural rather than neglected: deployment execution, production SLOs and dashboards, realistic-volume load evidence, cost right-sizing and the consent and residency process belong to the consuming apps, which is why §17, §29, §30 and §31 hold at 8 or below. The in-repo levers the backlog names are a single brand-hex token and an inline-style guard (Design System), the time-zone, error-code and default-email localization gaps (Internationalization), and consumer adoption of the shipped WASM payload budget (Front-End Performance); the other implementation-band categories have no lever identified yet.

    Top 5 risks

    1. FinOps execution and governance live downstream, §31 (impl 8, maturity 2, the lowest maturity on the board; a documented accepted cap per the backlog's Deliberate and accepted section): the framework ships real cost levers (an OTel Telemetry:TracesSampleRatio sampler knob, the outbox per-message log at Debug, OutboxPollFilterProcessor, and the default-ON probe-telemetry filter plus its span processor, Aspire/Extensions.Telemetry.cs:100,129, that keep health-probe traces and their SQL/Redis children out of ingestion), but right-sizing, reversible scale-events and per-service cost attribution are consumer/IaC concerns a library cannot perform, and no in-repo process or review enforces cost discipline.

      • Remediation: The in-repo telemetry-cost half is done. What remains is structural: the consumer apps must demonstrate measured right-sizing and per-service cost attribution that the framework's knobs feed, and a cost convention would have to be enforced somewhere (nothing under Tests/Architecture or in any workflow gates cost).
      • Expected delta: +1 maturity on §31 (2→3, +2 weighted) needs an enforced cost convention; 8→9 needs the consumer-side right-sizing and attribution evidence.

    2. Deployment gating is verified only downstream, §17 (maturity 3, impl 8, weight 2): a reference samples/deployment Bicep/OIDC sample + DEPLOYMENT.md ship and are compile-checked on every pull request, but that job is not a required context, and a library cannot self-deploy, so real CD-to-Azure lives in consumers; keyless nuget.org publishing under ADR-053 and the reviewer-gated release environment harden the release identity, not the deploy axis.

      • Remediation: A smoke-deploy of the samples/deployment sample, which moves §17 from referenced toward proven.
      • Expected delta: +1 impl on §17 (+2 weighted), and the maturity rung, remain available downstream.

    3. Compliance governance is consumer-owned; the in-repo guard verifies a mechanism, not the category, §30 (impl 8, maturity 3, weight 2): the headline §30 red flags are closed in-framework (the IAnonymizable erasure extension point, AES-256-GCM at rest, and the shipped, tested PiiRedactor masking [Pii] members before logs/telemetry), and PiiErasureContractFitnessTests forces a [Pii] type through PiiRedactor + IAnonymizable. PiiConventionTests adds a non-vacuous marking rule (Governance/PiiConventionTests.cs:10-14), PRIVACY.md ships in-repo, and the CSV export is fail-closed (EntityControllerBase.cs:231,272). The governing process (consent capture, lawful basis, residency verification) is delegated to consumers: consent has zero matches in Domain, Application and Infrastructure, and DataResidencyTestsBase has only its self-test probe in-repo, so the category as a whole is not automatically enforced.

      • Remediation: The in-repo mechanism is done; further maturity movement needs the consumer apps to carry the personal-data inventory + DSAR/consent/residency process the framework's [Pii]/IAnonymizable extension points feed (ADR-005).
      • Expected delta: +1 maturity on §30 (3→4, +2 weighted) is a consumer-side governance lift, not an in-repo one; the framework half is complete.

    4. Security holds at Implementation 8 on criteria that are unmet by decision, §11 (maturity 4, impl 8, weight 3, implPriority 3): authentication, authorization, rate limiting, brute-force protection, PBKDF2 hashing and security headers are strong and centralized, but the rubric-v2 written threat model is absent repo-wide and is recorded as not scheduled work by user decision, authorization is RBAC with capability indirection plus opt-in ownership rather than a resource/attribute policy engine. SECURITY.md:83-86 documents the shipped AddCommonKeyVaultConfiguration helper (a no-op unless KeyVault:Uri is set) and the sample's Key Vault URL plus managed-identity binding.

      • Remediation: No scheduled lever lifts the score: the threat model is an accepted, unscheduled gap.
      • Expected delta: No index movement is scheduled; 8→9 (+3 weighted) would need the threat model and a resource/attribute policy engine.

    5. Production continuity evidence is consumer-owned, §29 (maturity 4, impl 8, weight 3, implPriority 3): failure isolation is fitness-enforced and the in-repo restore drill is a build gate with a measured baseline, but the framework's own guide records production RTO/RPO, restores and SLO alerting as the consumer's (Website/docs-src/guides/common-RESILIENCE.md:5,31), with the SLO section an unfilled template (:95).

      • Remediation: Tested restores against real cloud backups, RTO/RPO per service and measured production SLOs in the consumer IaC; nothing further is schedulable in-repo.
      • Expected delta: +1 impl on §29 (+3 weighted) is a consumer-side lift.

    Top 5 strengths

    1. Dual-enforced Clean Architecture dependency rule (compile-time + fitness functions), §3 (impl 9): Source/Build/MMCA.Common.LayerEnforcement.targets:1-90 fails the build on layer violation (BeforeTargets ResolveProjectReferences); LayerDependencyTests.cs:12-58 and DomainPurityTests.cs:13-26 re-enforce Shared→Domain→Application→Infra→API/UI and Domain framework-freedom via NetArchTest. Reinforced by §34 (impl 8).

      • Remediation: Preserve by keeping both gates wired into CI and adding a regression test whenever a new layer/package is introduced; document the targets file in CLAUDE.md. To push the residual point, narrow the broader base-controller/UoW surfaces noted in §1.
      • Expected delta: Preserves impl 9 on §3; protects §34 from regression.

    2. Reference-quality test strategy (define-once fitness functions + an enforced coverage gate), §14 (impl 9): a right-shaped pyramid (~2,254 [Fact]/[Theory]) with executable architecture fitness functions (LayerDependency, DomainPurity, MicroserviceExtraction, DependencyVersion, PiiConvention, PiiErasureContract, AggregateConvention, EventVersioningConvention, FrameworkSanity, Specification, SliceCohesion) authored once in the shipped MMCA.Common.Testing.Architecture package (141 methods across 55 abstract bases) and re-run identically across all three repos (ADR-015), a zero-discovery min-tests guard, a shipped reusable E2E package, and a 68.3% line-coverage floor that fails the build (ci.yml:462, measured ~70.3%).

      • Remediation: Preserve by keeping the floor green and ratcheting it upward over releases; add mutation testing on the Core tier to reach a 10.
      • Expected delta: Holds §14 at impl 9; mutation testing on the Core tier is the remaining path toward 10; protects §28.

    3.       • Remediation: Automate the coordinated consumer version sweep (currently policy) to close the one remaining minor gap.
      • Expected delta: Automating the sweep nudges §32 toward fully exemplary; preserves impl 9.

    4. Centralized, alg-pinned security with shipped anti-automation defenses and PBKDF2 hashing, §11 (impl 8): WebApplicationBuilderExtensions.cs:364,380 pins ValidAlgorithms per RS256/HS256 (defeats alg-confusion) with full issuer/audience/lifetime/signing-key validation, :217 extends the RS256 pin to the JWKS-forwarded path; :34 rate-limit bypass for health/.well-known/grpc; LoginProtectionService.cs:50-56,74-82 adds exponential brute-force lockout + per-IP registration limit (ADR-029); PasswordHasher.cs:9,53 hashes with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 (600k iterations, PBKDF2-only, FixedTimeEquals, ADR-032): all returning Result.Failure rather than throwing.

      • Remediation: Keep the CI vulnerable-package gate green.
      • Expected delta: Preserves impl 8; the threat model that 8→9 needs is an accepted, unscheduled gap (see risk 4).

    5. Architecture-test-enforced microservices extraction boundaries + event versioning, §7 (maturity 4, impl 9): ADR-006 (database-per-service), transport-agnostic IMessageBus.cs:28, and the shared Rules/Layering/ArchitectureRules.Transport.cs:19 rule (subclassed through Bases/Layering/MicroserviceExtractionTestsBase.cs:13 in MicroserviceExtractionTests.cs:11, run at ci.yml:161 under a required check) forbidding Application/Domain/Shared→MassTransit and Grpc→Domain/Application/Infrastructure. The rubric-v2 modernization-patterns criterion is met in shipped docs (Grpc/DependencyInjection.cs:21,71,78, 007-grpc-extraction.md:23, 008-service-extraction-topology.md:41).

      • Remediation: Preserve the naming by keeping the ACL and Strangler Fig wording in the Grpc XML docs and in ADR-007/008 whenever either is revised (no drift gate watches the naming itself); to push the residual point, demonstrate one end-to-end lift-out (the Notifications module) in a sample to convert "extractable by design" into "extracted and proven".
      • Expected delta: 9→10 on §7 needs a proven extraction, which is structurally consumer-side.
